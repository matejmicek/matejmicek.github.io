T/The thesis · 60 seconds

The most contested real estate in agent infra.
Incumbents keep the revenue, concede the argument.

Twelve weeks turned "promising picks-and-shovels" into a knife fight: violent re-ratings at the top, hyperscalers landing all at once, labs buying the layer outright, and a vocabulary war ("sandbox" → "computer") that predicts roadmaps better than funding announcements do.

Where value is accruing
Modal ($300M ARR, sandboxes >⅓ and +40–50% MoM), Prime Intellect ($0→$100M ARR→$1B), Daytona (74% MoM, RL = 50% of usage), E2B (the long-tail commons: Manus, Genspark, Anything…), credential brokering (Cisco paid ~$300M for Astrix).
Where it isn't
The raw microVM primitive (Tencent + Alibaba give E2B-compatible runtimes away free), computer-use pure-plays (sub-$1M funded; Scrapybara already pivoted out), WASM (Fermyon absorbed into Akamai), zombies & casualties: Morph, Riza, Val Town's −1% ARR month.
Three questions worth a call this week
1 · Browserbase: discipline or unconverted ARR? Klein is on tape working "my Series C." 2 · Does any RL-env vendor have durable non-lab revenue? 3 · Are Anthropic/Devin "provider slots" distribution — or pre-commoditization?
Δ/What actually changed · Feb–Aug 2026

The six deltas that matter.

Everything below is new relative to the May map — sourced, dated, and verified.

  • Δ1 · The hyperscaler wave landed all at once. Cloudflare (Sandboxes GA Apr 13 → @cloudflare/computer → Kitesurf, a from-scratch non-Chromium Rust agent browser, Aug 7); Google (GKE Agent Sandbox GA, 16x growth in 5 months + Agent Substrate, which schedules around Kubernetes); AWS (AgentCore Harness GA + an entirely new Lambda MicroVMs product whose first reference build was Claude self-hosted sandboxes); Azure; CoreWeave; Docker (custom VMM, biggest HN thread of the year, 693 pts — still no Linux). The "hyperscalers won't compete here" thesis is dead in its broad form. Bonus: AWS, Google and Vercel disclosed the same authorization-bypass CVE class together in August ("CoreBreak", up to CVSS 9.3).
  • Δ2 · RL environments: footnote → the biggest new funded category. 35–60 tracked vendors. Lab-facing: Mechanize ($9.1M at $500M post → Google $1.5B+ license-and-hire talks 103 days later), Fleet (in talks ~$750M on $1M→$60M ARR), Deeptune ($43M a16z → acquired by Mercor in 4 months). Full-stack: Prime Intellect ($130M at $1B, $100M ARR, 6,000 customers), Applied Compute (launch → $1.3B in <10 months). Daytona says RL/eval went 0% → ~50% of its usage. The bear case: 5–6 lab buyers, and license-and-hire exits return talent without cap-table multiples.
  • Δ3 · "Sandbox" lost the argument; sandboxes kept the money. Casado (a16z, backing Runta $20M seed): "AI agents just want a computer… this isn't yet another sandbox cloud." Fly.io: "The age of sandboxes is over." Yet Fly raised $25M while Modal raised $355M, and the fork-the-warm-VM economy (850K sandboxes/day at one Daytona customer, Modal's 50K launches/sec, forkd's 100 children in 101ms) shows the market buying faster disposability. E2B's homepage now literally says "computers for agents" — concede the framing, absorb the features, keep the revenue.
  • Δ4 · The open-source flip. Daytona went closed-source June 11 ("we are handing the exact blueprint… to an AI that never gets tired") — HN verdict: "security by obscurity"; star flow went net-negative; repo archived. One month later E2B declared itself "the only major open-source agentic sandbox platform" and paired Apache-2.0 with EU-AI-Act copy the month the Act went live. Meanwhile Tencent CubeSandbox (E2B-SDK drop-in, 0→11K stars in 4 months) and Alibaba-linked OpenSandbox attack from below — and GitHub killed the public stargazer-history API, breaking the ecosystem's own measuring stick.
  • Δ5 · The biggest fish keep leaving the pond. Perplexity built SPACE in-house in 10 weeks; Anthropic runs its own gVisor containers + native hypervisors; OpenAI bought Ona; Cognition built otterlink/blockdiff (30-minute EC2 snapshots → seconds); Replit runs its own Nix stack. The same players run the biggest partner programs for everyone else (Claude Managed Agents, Devin Outposts, Stripe Projects) — the vendor commons wins the long tail of the agent economy, not its top.
  • Δ6 · Security became the plot — and the fear sells the wrong product. The OpenClaw saga ran all year (first-ever agentic-system CVE → 135K exposed instances, 138+ CVEs → gVisor's MAGI architecture → Coder's $90M KKR round). But every 2026 "escape" (Kimi K3, OpenAI/HF, Claude Code) was config/policy failure, not isolation failure — UK AISI found zero novel exploits. The recurring bug class is authorization, which is why credential/egress brokering (Infisical Agent Vault, Kernel Managed Auth, nono, Runta) is the fastest-emerging layer, with Cisco×Astrix (~$250–350M) as the proven exit.

X/Head-to-head · where core beliefs collide

Twelve live conflicts.

Named sides, verbatim claims, and what would settle each — this is where the next 12 months of roadmaps are visible today.

  • Sandbox vs. full computer. Casado/a16z: "agents just want a computer… not yet another sandbox cloud" vs. E2B/Modal's revenue dominance. Words moved to Casado, money stayed put. Settled when persistent line-items become the majority of incumbent revenue.
  • Is ephemeral obsolete? Mackey/Fly: "The age of sandboxes is over" vs. 850K sandboxes/day, 50K launches/sec, 101ms 100-child forks. Workloads split: RL is structurally ephemeral; office agents accumulate state. OpenAI buying Ona is the strongest lab vote for persistence.
  • Closed vs. open source — same product, same quarter. Daytona: "we are handing the exact blueprint… to an AI that never gets tired" vs. E2B, one month later: "the only major open-source agentic sandbox platform." HN sided with E2B; commerce hasn't voted. Watch for a regulated-enterprise win citing auditability.
  • Firecracker vs. gVisor. AWS: "I don't think anybody else has the level of security we do" vs. Modal at $4.65B on gVisor. ⚠️ The "gVisor escaped 18% of AISI runs" stat is unverified against the primary paper. Pluggable backends are winning — favoring orchestration over primitive loyalty.
  • Kubernetes: extend or bypass. Burazin: "Kubernetes is not your sandbox" vs. GKE Agent Sandbox GA — and Google shipped the bypass itself (Agent Substrate). Physics favors bypass; distribution favors K8s.
  • Browser infra: standalone, feature, or OSS-vertical. Klein's icky-parts moat vs. Cloudflare literally building the browser vs. browser-use at $0.02/hr — plus Unikraft's public rebuttal of browser-use's migration story on its own launch thread. Klein's rename musing is the tell.
  • RL environments: 3–5 winners, 60-vendor froth, or lab absorption. Wing's consolidation call is being executed by absorption (Mercor×Deeptune, Google×Mechanize) ahead of schedule — while OpenAI's own RFT "falling short" argues labs can't fully insource. Non-lab revenue is the scarce proof.
  • "Execution is commoditizing" vs. 24x funding growth. Both true, split by layer: the primitive is free (Tencent gives it away); the $504M buys everything around it. Vasek: "running sandboxes fast — that's not even half the story."
  • Linux vs. Windows vs. macOS as the agent desktop. Linux for headless fleets; Windows is where enterprise work lives (Daytona GA'd it, OpenAI built a from-scratch Windows sandbox, Microsoft launched Windows 365 for Agents); macOS capped by Apple's EULA yet a top-3 star magnet for local agents.
  • Do stars still matter? E2B flat stars / booming logos; Daytona negative stars / 74% MoM; vercel/sandbox 183 stars / 100+ releases — vs. browser-use and CubeSandbox riding stars into real position. Refined: stars = distribution & standards signal, no longer a moat signal.
  • Escapes: existential or theater? Every 2026 incident was policy/config, not primitive failure (AISI: zero novel exploits) — but the market consequence is real: the recurring bug class is authorization, so the winning product is an egress proxy, not a thicker VM.
  • "Labs won't own the icky parts" — dead or alive? Dead broadly (Ona, Lambda MicroVMs, Kitesurf, Docker). Alive in one redoubt: no lab ships CAPTCHA-solving/stealth. Live question: are provider slots durable distribution or pre-commoditization?
Meta-pattern: across all twelve, the incumbents keep winning the revenue while conceding the argument. Watch the vocabulary migrations — they predict product roadmaps twelve months out better than the funding announcements do.

★/Who wins each layer · the scorecard

Who wins each category.

The partner-meeting answer, layer by layer.

  • Code-execution sandboxes: Modal is winning the money game, E2B the ecosystem game, Daytona the feature-velocity game. The primitive is commoditized — back compounding wedges (SDK-as-standard + BYOC; Windows/GPU + RL). Undifferentiated speed plays die against free Tencent/Alibaba clones.
  • Hyperscaler runtimes: AWS executes best, Google thinks best (Agent Substrate). They take the compliance-default segment; independents keep multi-cloud neutrality + the graduation path from lab-managed agents.
  • Browser infra: standalone for now under a three-front squeeze. Kernel has the best wedge-per-dollar (identity + the Anthropic slot); Browserbase's Series C print is the category referendum.
  • Computer-use: a feature, not a category — except the Windows wedge (licensing economics + lab benchmark demand) and identity-heavy desktop RPA. Cua is the asymmetric lottery ticket.
  • RL environments: barbell. Own the open standard + enterprise breadth (Prime Intellect) or bespoke lab trust (expect license-and-hire exits). The middle — content shops — gets absorbed by Mercor/Scale/Surge.
  • Snapshot/branching: real technical differentiation, features-not-companies risk everywhere. Namespace's vendor-of-vendors position is the one to watch if it diversifies.
  • Security/credential brokering: the best new-wedge risk/reward on this map — it fixes the bug class that actually recurs (authorization), and Cisco×Astrix proved the exit.
  • Durable orchestration: Temporal holds the top; the middle gets squeezed by free platform bundles (eve, Cloudflare Agents SDK). Independent DX plays must become multi-cloud defaults before the bundles close the gap.
